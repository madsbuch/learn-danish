**[[say: at være]]** means *to be*. In the present tense it is **[[say: er]]**, whatever the subject:

| Danish | English |
|---|---|
| [[say: jeg er]] | I am |
| [[say: du er]] | you are |
| [[say: han er]] / [[say: hun er]] | he is / she is |
| [[say: vi er]] | we are |
| [[say: I er]] | you are (plural) |
| [[say: de er]] | they are |

Danish normally **keeps the subject pronoun**. Notice that plural **I** is always a capital letter.

### Who and what
- [[say: Jeg er Anna.]] — I am Anna.
- [[say: Jeg er lærer.]] — I am a teacher. A profession after *er* usually has no article.
- [[say: Det er mit hus.]] — It is my house.
- [[say: Vi er hjemme.]] — We are at home.

### Questions and negatives
Put the verb first in a yes/no question: [[say: Er du hjemme?]] — Are you at home?
In a simple main clause, **ikke** follows the finite verb: [[say: Jeg er ikke hjemme.]] — I am not at home.

Adjectives may change for gender and number: [[say: Han er træt.]] — He is tired; [[say: Vi er trætte.]] — We are tired.
