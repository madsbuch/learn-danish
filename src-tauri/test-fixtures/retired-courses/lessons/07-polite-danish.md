Use **[[say: du]]** for one person and **[[say: I]]** for more than one. Capital **I** means *you (plural)*; lowercase **i** means *in*. **[[say: de]]** means *they*.

Danish usually uses *du* even with adults you have just met. The formal pronoun **De** exists but is uncommon in ordinary conversation.

### Friendly requests
- [[say: Jeg vil gerne have en kop kaffe.]] — I would like a cup of coffee.
- [[say: Må jeg få regningen?]] — May I have the bill?
- [[say: Kan du hjælpe mig?]] — Can you help me?
- [[say: En kop te, tak.]] — A cup of tea, please.

There is no single Danish word used exactly like English *please* in every situation. A question, *gerne*, and a friendly tone often make a request polite. **Tak** can mean *thank you* or the *please* at the end of an order.

### When you are learning
- [[say: Undskyld, jeg forstår ikke.]] — Sorry, I do not understand.
- [[say: Kan du sige det igen?]] — Can you say that again?
- [[say: Vil du tale lidt langsommere?]] — Would you speak a little more slowly?
- [[say: Jeg er ved at lære dansk.]] — I am learning Danish.

Danish has the letters **æ, ø, å**. Listen to the audio for whole words. In typing drills, this app accepts **ae, oe, aa** when you cannot enter those letters.
