In a Danish **main-clause statement**, the finite verb is in the second position. A position can contain several words.

- [[say: Jeg drikker kaffe i dag.]] — I am drinking coffee today.
- [[say: I dag drikker jeg kaffe.]] — Today I am drinking coffee.

When *i dag* comes first, the verb still occupies the second position, and the subject comes after it.

### Questions
For yes/no questions, begin with the verb:
- [[say: Drikker du kaffe?]] — Do you drink coffee?
- [[say: Er hun hjemme?]] — Is she at home?

With a question word, use question word + verb + subject:
- [[say: Hvor bor du?]] — Where do you live?
- [[say: Hvad spiser de?]] — What are they eating?

### Negation
In ordinary main clauses, **ikke** normally follows the finite verb and subject:
- [[say: Jeg drikker ikke kaffe.]] — I do not drink coffee.
- [[say: I dag drikker jeg ikke kaffe.]] — Today I am not drinking coffee.
- [[say: Jeg kan ikke komme.]] — I cannot come.

In a subordinate clause introduced by *fordi*, *ikke* comes before the finite verb:
[[say: Jeg bliver hjemme, fordi jeg ikke er rask.]] — I am staying at home because I am not well.

Practise short main clauses first, then compare the two positions of *ikke*.
