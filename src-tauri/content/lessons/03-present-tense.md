Most Danish verbs form the present tense by adding **-r** to the infinitive. An infinitive is often introduced by **at** (*to*).

| Infinitive | Present | Example |
|---|---|---|
| [[say: at tale]] — to speak | [[say: taler]] | [[say: Jeg taler dansk.]] — I speak Danish. |
| [[say: at spise]] — to eat | [[say: spiser]] | [[say: Vi spiser nu.]] — We are eating now. |
| [[say: at bo]] — to live | [[say: bor]] | [[say: Hun bor i Aarhus.]] — She lives in Aarhus. |
| [[say: at læse]] — to read | [[say: læser]] | [[say: De læser en bog.]] — They are reading a book. |

The form does **not** change for person or number: [[say: jeg taler]], [[say: du taler]], [[say: vi taler]].
Danish has no separate everyday present-progressive form: *jeg læser* can mean both *I read* and *I am reading*.

### Learn these common forms
- [[say: at være]] → [[say: er]] — to be
- [[say: at have]] → [[say: har]] — to have
- [[say: at gøre]] → [[say: gør]] — to do
- [[say: at gå]] → [[say: går]] — to walk / go

After a modal verb, use the infinitive **without at**: [[say: Jeg kan tale dansk.]] — I can speak Danish.
Do not add another *-r*: [[say: Vi skal spise.]] — We are going to eat.
