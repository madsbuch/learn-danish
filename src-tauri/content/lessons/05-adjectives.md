Many Danish adjectives follow a three-form pattern: the basic form, **-t** for neuter singular, and **-e** for plural or definite noun phrases.

| Common gender | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| [[say: en rød bil]] — a red car | [[say: et rødt hus]] — a red house | [[say: røde biler]] — red cars |
| [[say: en stor hund]] — a big dog | [[say: et stort hus]] — a big house | [[say: store huse]] — big houses |

The same agreement usually applies after *er*:
- [[say: Bilen er rød.]] — The car is red.
- [[say: Huset er rødt.]] — The house is red.
- [[say: Husene er røde.]] — The houses are red.

### The red car
Use **den / det / de** plus the *-e* adjective and a noun without a definite suffix:
- [[say: den røde bil]] — the red car
- [[say: det røde hus]] — the red house
- [[say: de røde biler]] — the red cars

### Important exceptions
Not every adjective adds these endings. [[say: et godt svar]] — a good answer, uses the irregular neuter of *god*. *Lille* has an irregular plural: [[say: en lille bil]], [[say: et lille hus]], [[say: små huse]]. Learn exceptions as phrases.
