Danish nouns have two grammatical genders: **common gender** with **en**, and **neuter** with **et**. Gender is not the same as biological sex. Learn each noun together with its article.

| A / an | The |
|---|---|
| [[say: en bil]] — a car | [[say: bilen]] — the car |
| [[say: en pige]] — a girl | [[say: pigen]] — the girl |
| [[say: et hus]] — a house | [[say: huset]] — the house |
| [[say: et æble]] — an apple | [[say: æblet]] — the apple |

Without an adjective, the definite article attaches to the end: usually **-en** or **-et**. Nouns already ending in an unstressed *-e* add only **-n** or **-t**.

### More than one
There are several plural patterns. Learn the plural with the noun:
- [[say: en bil, to biler]] — one car, two cars
- [[say: en hund, to hunde]] — one dog, two dogs
- [[say: et hus, to huse]] — one house, two houses
- [[say: et barn, to børn]] — one child, two children (irregular)

The definite plural is commonly **-ne** after a plural ending: [[say: bilerne]] — the cars; [[say: husene]] — the houses. Some forms are irregular: [[say: børnene]] — the children.

With an adjective, use a separate definite article instead: [[say: den røde bil]] — the red car; [[say: det røde hus]] — the red house.
